SideBell · User Guide

One app, two devices, each playing one role
中文 · English

The caregiver sets up both devices; the patient only has to tap a cell. The two chapters are independent — read the one for whichever device is in your hands. Set up the patient side first, so the connection comes up while you are setting up the second device.

SideBell is a care assistance calling tool. It cannot replace emergency medical alert services. In an emergency, call your local emergency number. It works over Bluetooth, so its range is limited; it cannot send calls when the app is closed or the device runs out of battery.

Before you start

  • Two Apple devices — one beside the bed (an iPad works best; the cells are large and easy to hit), one carried by the caregiver (usually an iPhone).
  • SideBell installed on both. It is the same app; each device picks its role after installation.
  • They cannot be too far apart. Only testing in your own home will tell you the real range — walls and floors both matter. Once you are set up, walk around and try it from the places you actually use.
Not needed: internet, Wi-Fi, an account, a subscription — and no pairing in iOS Settings → Bluetooth. SideBell finds the other device by itself.

Chapter 1 · Patient side

The device beside the patient. Once set up it stays on, and the patient only taps cells.

1Confirm the notice, allow Bluetooth

Open SideBell. The two role cards at the bottom are greyed out and will not respond.

Tick “I understand the notice above” first. Only then do the cards become tappable. Tapping a card before that does nothing — it is not frozen.

The moment you tick it, the Bluetooth request appears.

The patient side needs only this one permission. The caregiver side also asks for notifications.

2Choose the role

Choose Patient. If you pick the wrong one, you can switch it later in Settings.

3The main screen

The connection state is in the top-left corner. The screenshot above shows it once both devices are ready. At this point in your own setup the caregiver side does not exist yet, so it will read Not connected in orange — that is expected. It turns green once the second device is done.

One tap sends a call. There is no confirmation step. If sending a call took two taps or a long press, someone who can only use Eye Tracking could not call for help.

4Add the items this home needs

Only Discomfort and Water are there by default. Turn over, suction, bathroom, air conditioning, just want to talk — every household needs something different, and you can add your own.

Opening Settings takes two taps

Tapping Settings in the top-right corner once turns it into an orange Tap again. A second tap within three seconds actually opens it.

This exists to block accidental activation by Eye Tracking — a patient who lands in Settings by mistake may not be able to get back out on their own.

Edit call items

“Discomfort” is always first and cannot be removed. It is the urgent call, and the caregiver’s alert behaves differently for it — see Two rules.

Adding an item

Name items the way the patient would say them. These words are read aloud by the caregiver’s phone — “Turn over” beats “Reposition”, “Can’t breathe well” beats “Respiratory distress”.

Reordering and removing

After adding

Start with three or four cells. The more cells there are, the smaller each one gets, and that is a real barrier for someone with limited hand control or using Eye Tracking. Live with it for a while before deciding what else is genuinely needed.

5After a cell is tapped

One of three states appears under the cell:

“No response” does not mean “not delivered”. The call may well have reached the caregiver’s phone and set off the alert — they were simply asleep or out of the room. What it means for the patient is the same either way: this route is not working right now, try another way to get help.

For someone who cannot ask a second time, knowing whether they were heard matters as much as the call itself. The state is written on the cell; nobody has to be asked.

Chapter 2 · Caregiver side

The device the caregiver carries, usually a phone. This is where calls ring.

1Confirm the notice, allow Bluetooth

Same as the patient side: tick “I understand the notice above” before the role cards become tappable.

2Choose the role

Choose Caregiver.

3Allow notifications

Once the call list opens, iOS asks a second time. The caregiver side needs this one extra permission.

Notifications must be allowed. When the phone is locked and in a pocket, notifications are how a call surfaces. Without them, you only see calls if the app happens to be open.

4Check that they are connected

No pairing, no codes, and nothing to find in iOS Settings → Bluetooth (it is not listed there, and that is correct). With both apps open and the devices close enough, they connect on their own.

Check the patient side too — its top-left corner should have turned green as well. Both devices green means it is genuinely working. If it is still orange after a minute, see If something is wrong.

5When a call arrives

The phone raises an alarm and says the item name aloud, so you know what is needed without looking at the screen.

If something is wrong

It keeps saying “Not connected”

  1. Is SideBell open on both devices? Swiping the app away from the app switcher stops it working.
  2. Is Bluetooth on for both? Check iOS Control Centre.
  3. Too far apart? Put the devices side by side first to confirm they connect, then move them apart to find the range that actually works in your home.
  4. Did both pick the same role? Two Caregiver devices will never connect. Check the Role row in Settings.

It stays on “Waiting”

The call went out, but the caregiver has not tapped “Got it” yet. After three minutes it becomes “No response”.

“Don’t Allow” was tapped by mistake

iOS only asks once, and the app will never prompt again. Open iOS Settings, scroll down to SideBell, and turn Bluetooth on — plus Notifications on the caregiver side.

Changing devices or swapping roles

Tap the red Switch role in Settings. Switching roles stops the current connection, so check both devices again afterwards.
